## 2a. Wang et al., arXiv 2402.01349v3 (6 Dec 2024), "LLMs May Perform MCQA by Selecting the Least Incorrect Option" (Wang, Zhao, Qiang, Xi, Qin, Liu; HIT)

Full text read (PDF). Our sentence: Wang et al. "present all-wrong multiple-choice questions with a no-answer option; they do not compare against isolated verification of the same options".

Relevant passages:
- Sec 3.3 (Transforming to True-or-False Format): "We transform the original MCQAs in only MMLU and MedMCQA into a True-or-False (T/F) format ... we generate T/F-format questions, including one with the correct option (T/F: correct) and other questions with the incorrect options (T/F: incorrect)". Result: "LLaMA 3 70B achieves an accuracy as low as 36.8% on the 'T/F: incorrect' datasets based on MedMCQA" and "they frequently misclassify statements containing incorrect options as correct."
- Sec 4.1 (Option Probability Analysis): "we convert MCQs into T/F format ... For a specific MCQ, we consider the incorrect* option with the highest confidence in corresponding T/F questions for C_incorrect*." Result: "the incorrect* options still achieve substantial confidence ranging from 83.6% to 99.4% of those for the correct options."
- Sec 4.2 (MCQA with No Correct Option): "we prompt the LLMs with 'If you think there is no correct answer, you can respond with "no answer"' ... we replace the correct options with non-semantic tokens ... ChatGPT-4o successfully identifies that there is no correct answer in approximately 60% of the MCQs. However, for the remaining 40% of the questions, it still selects one option as correct".
- Sec 3.4 also tests "None of the above" replacing the correct option.

Verdict: **needs rewording; as written it is inaccurate.** (i) The all-wrong setting with a no-answer option is accurate (Sec 4.2; also 3.4). (ii) "do not compare against isolated verification of the same options" is WRONG as stated: Wang et al. do run isolated T/F verification of the incorrect options (Sec 3.3, 4.1) and relate it to MCQA choice. What they do not do: (a) compare the forced-choice pick in an all-wrong pool with the option that isolated verification accepts most often, in the same pool, as an equality or rate test (4.1 uses the max-confidence incorrect option, but only on questions with a correct option present and where the model already answered correctly); (b) compare a pooled-with-NONE rate against isolated acceptance rate on unsolvable problems (4.2 reports only the no-answer rate, with no isolated baseline); (c) use model-generated candidate pools or best-of-N. Suggested rewording: "Wang et al. show that LLMs frequently accept individual incorrect options in isolated true/false form and often select an option when the correct answer is replaced by non-semantic tokens despite a 'no answer' instruction; they do not test, within all-wrong pools, whether the forced choice coincides with the candidate that the same model's isolated verification accepts most often, nor compare pooled NONE-option behaviour with isolated acceptance on problems the judge cannot solve."

## 2b. Ji et al., arXiv 2608.25937v2 (30 Aug 2026), "Candidate supply and answer selection shape the value of LLM judging in multi-agent systems"

Full text read (PDF). Our sentence: Ji et al. "study how candidate supply shapes the value of LLM judging but exclude all-wrong pools".

Relevant passages:
- Fig. 1c legend: "No selection rule can recover a correct answer when no correct candidate was generated (p_gen = 0 ...)".
- Results, pool-composition stratification: "judge selection helped most when correct candidates formed a small minority (0 < p_pool < 0.25). All-wrong pools were excluded (Fig. 4c)."
- Methods: "Pools containing only correct or only wrong traces were excluded from rank AUC calculations because they contained no correct-wrong comparisons."
- Abstract: "(1) A correct answer is often already present among the generated candidates, but the system can still converge on and report a wrong answer."

Verdict: **accurate but could be more precise.** The all-wrong pools are excluded from the stratified and rank-AUC analyses, and the paper treats them only as a "no selection rule can recover" regime. It does not analyse which wrong candidate gets chosen. Optional precision: "study how candidate supply shapes the value of LLM judging, but exclude all-wrong pools from their stratified and ranking analyses (treating them as unrecoverable)." Limitation: I did not exhaustively read the HLE sections or extended data. grep for all-wrong, no correct and exclud found only the passages above.
